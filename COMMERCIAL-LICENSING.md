# Commercial licensing and public repository policy

**Status:** Initial policy; legal review required before external commercial distribution.
**Owner:** Tinlance Limited
**Repository visibility:** Public by deliberate product strategy.

## 1. Public visibility is not an open-source grant

The repository is public so prospective customers, evaluators and contributors can inspect the implementation and discuss it. Public visibility alone does not grant a general right to use, modify, deploy, white-label, redistribute, sublicense or resell the software. The repository's LICENSE file states the current reserved-rights position.

Do not describe TLMS as open source unless Tinlance later adopts an OSI-approved licence after a rights and dependency review.

## 2. Commercial permissions are contractually scoped

Tinlance may offer separate written agreements for evaluation and proof-of-concept use; internal commercial deployment; production deployment; modification and customization; white-label branding; redistribution and sublicensing; managed hosting and support; enterprise deployment and service levels.

A permission in one category must not be inferred from another. White-label and redistribution rights must be explicit, including permitted territory, duration, number of deployments, customer sublicensing, attribution, support obligations, updates, termination and post-termination rights.

## 3. Product editions

The proposed Community, Professional and White-Label/Enterprise editions are planning concepts, not current offers or granted rights. Feature boundaries, prices, support commitments and legal terms require approval before sale.

## 4. Contributions and intellectual property

Until a contribution agreement is approved, do not accept code contributions that would create unclear ownership or incompatible licensing obligations. Contributors must identify third-party code, generated code, copied snippets, assets and dependencies. Do not submit confidential or employer-owned material.

Before enabling external contributions, Tinlance must choose and publish a contributor agreement or CLA/DCO policy, define copyright ownership and review the legal consequences. No contributor should be asked to transfer rights without a clear, reviewed agreement.

## 5. Third-party components

Every dependency and asset must be inventoried with exact version, source, licence identifier, notices and any attribution/source-disclosure obligations. Copyleft, network-copyleft, non-commercial, source-available and field-of-use terms require legal review before adoption. Preserve required notices.

## 6. Trademarks

The Tinlance and TLMS names, logos and marks are not licensed by permission to view this repository. See TRADEMARKS.md.

## 7. Release gate

No commercial release may be published until ownership and contributor rights are documented; all dependency and asset licences are reviewed; the customer agreement and edition matrix are approved; white-label and redistribution rights are precisely defined; and privacy, security, support and warranty terms are reviewed by counsel.

This document records engineering policy and is not legal advice or a customer contract.
